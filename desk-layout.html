Control desk — the agreed layout

The whole Desk as decided, before it is built. It is drawn with the shipped tile, pill and Needs-action styles (the ones court-board-states.html mirrors), so only what is new is new: the clock in the header, the day bar's pace line, and the Up next panel that replaces Next transitions. One moment runs through every picture: Saturday, 14:37, eight courts — the same story the landing page tells.

Equal heights

2b. Few courts, many courts

The two columns are always the same height because neither sets it: the screen does. The desk takes the window under the day bar and the page never scrolls; each column scrolls inside itself. What changes with the court count is only where the spare room or the overflow goes.

New panel

3. Up next, row by row

Every row sits on one grid. On the left, when the court frees — the sort key, so the times line up and read as a timeline, with the published time under it only when it differs. In the middle, the court and its event, the names to call (the one bold line), and a status line that is always the third line: ✓ Players free, ! who to chase, or players not known yet. On the right, the bell, top-aligned. "Players free" is about people; "Ready" stays the court's word, on the tile. Whether players are free is judged when the court frees — not now. There is one time per row and it says what it is: "frees ~14:55" is an estimate from the slot, never a countdown; the published time sits under it, muted, only when it differs. A busy court's tile names its next pairing without a time, so the two never show different numbers for one match. The whole row opens the match. The bell beside it is Notify players: drawn now, inactive until player notifications exist. It is quiet and out of the Tab order, and the panel header says once "Notify players: coming soon". The call is made at the desk until then. There is no "called" mark.

Imperfect wifi

4b. When the connection drops

The badge is the shipped one: Live, Connecting, Offline, live updates paused. A quiet screen that is connected is healthy, so age alone never recolours it. What this adds is what the rest of the page does once updates stop: a bar under the header that says since when, and every minute and time on the page marked as of that moment, so a moderator never acts on a number that looks current and is not.

Screens

4d. Which screens the Desk is built for

Chosen from what desk laptops actually run, not from a guess. What matters is the viewport - the page's own pixels after display scaling, the browser's tabs and address bar and the taskbar - not the screen's resolution. A 1920×1080 laptop at the usual 125% scaling is a 1536×864 screen to the page, and about 1536×700 inside a maximised browser.

Full screen (F11) gives back that space.

The tournament header has to scroll away. Above the Desk the workspace header (brand, tournament name, section tabs) and the page title take about 260px. A console fixed to the window would lose that for good, leaving a 1366×620 laptop under 400px for both lists. So the console is exactly one viewport tall and sits below that header: one scroll - or the page opening scrolled to it - and the whole window is the Desk.
Lists that move

4e. Holding still under the pointer, and saying what is new

Needs action and Up next re-rank themselves every 30 seconds and on every live update. Two rules keep that from costing the moderator a wrong click or a missed alert.

RuleWhat happensWhy
Hold the order under the pointer While the pointer is over a list, or focus is inside it, its rows keep their order. Their numbers and wording still update in place. A row that should leave stays, dimmed, until the hold ends; a new row is added at the end of the held list. When the pointer leaves or focus moves out, the list settles into its new order in one short move. A re-sort under the pointer turns a click aimed at Court 4's Start into a click on another court's. On a button that writes, that is the worst mistake the Desk can make.
Mark what is new A row that was not in the list a moment ago gets a "New" tag and a tinted edge for 10 seconds, then looks like any other row. The browser tab's title carries the count of Needs-action rows - "(3) Control desk" - so it is visible from another tab. No sound by default. The moderator looks at the courts far more than at the screen. One quiet, bounded cue; never a blinking row, which would compete with every alert after it.
Narrow screens

5. Phone and the read-only view

Narrow, the panels stack with the work lists first — Needs action, then Up next, then the board — because eight tiles in one column would push the queue off the screen. A caller without can_edit still gets only the day picker and the board: neither list asks them to do anything.

7. Assumptions

No tournament has run on the app yet. These are chosen, not measured, and the first tournament's own match times (scheduled, started, ended) are enough to replay the day and check them — no extra tracking.
